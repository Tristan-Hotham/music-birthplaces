Where the world's music comes from

Wikidata, the open database behind Wikipedia, knows {{source.n|int}} musicians, singers, composers, rappers and DJs, and {{source.g_n|int}} bands, choirs and orchestras. This is where they were born and where the bands began: Vienna's composers and Nashville's country singers, Kingston's reggae, Seoul's K-pop and the black metal of Oslo and Bergen, decade by decade.

Every musician was born somewhere, and every band started somewhere. Put {{source.n_acts_pt|int}} of those places on one map and the history of music appears in the shape of the world: the courts and conservatories of central Europe, the port cities of the Atlantic, the American South, Kingston, Rio, Tokyo and Seoul. Some music is everywhere. Some, like country, reggae, tango or K-pop, has a home town.

It is also a map of who has been written down. Wikidata knows where {{source.notable_bp_pct|f1}}% of famous musicians were born, but only {{source.sl1_bp_pct|pc}} of those with one Wikipedia article or none, and it knows where just {{source.g_pt_pct|pc}} of its bands were formed. So read it as the open record of music, not a census. Switch between musicians and bands, choose a kind of musician or a family of genres, drag the decade slider (decade of birth, or of formation for bands), and click a hexagon for the people and bands from there, best known first.

    02 · CitiesNew York, London, Paris and Vienna

    Group every birthplace within 25 km of a city of 200,000 people or more, so that Brooklyn counts with New York and Hollywood with Los Angeles, and {{areas.top.0.name|raw}} comes first with {{areas.top.0.n|int}} musicians, then {{areas.top.1.name|raw}} ({{areas.top.1.n|int}}), {{areas.top.2.name|raw}} ({{areas.top.2.n|int}}) and {{areas.top.3.name|raw}} ({{areas.top.3.n|int}}). Taken as a single Wikidata place, Vienna is the most common birthplace of all ({{places.top.0.n|int}}). Tokyo ({{areas.tokyo.n|int}}) is the first city outside Europe and the Americas.

    Musicians born in each city, and bands formed there

    Places within 25 km of a Natural Earth city of 200,000 or more. Per head: per 100,000 people living there today, cities with {{areas.per_capita_min_n|int}}+ musicians or {{areas.per_capita_b_min_n|int}}+ bands. Hover a bar.

    Per head the old capitals of central Europe pull away. Vienna has {{areas.vienna.per100k|f1}} musicians in Wikidata for every 100,000 people who live there now, Prague {{areas.prague.per100k|f1}}, against New York's {{areas.new_york.per100k|f1}} and Tokyo's {{areas.tokyo.per100k|f1}}. The very top of the per-head list, {{areas.per_capita.0.name|raw}} ({{areas.per_capita.0.per100k|f1}}), {{areas.per_capita.1.name|raw}} and {{areas.per_capita.2.name|raw}}, says as much about national libraries as about music: Estonian and Czech catalogues of composers and performers have been copied into Wikidata in bulk.

    Bands are a different map. Los Angeles is where most bands in Wikidata were formed ({{areas.los_angeles.b|int}}), ahead of New York ({{areas.new_york.b|int}}) and London ({{areas.london.b|int}}), then San Francisco and Seattle. Per head, the band towns are Ljubljana, Bergen, Malmö and Oslo, with Liverpool and Nashville not far behind.

    03 · Two mapsComposers' Europe and the pop and rock world

    Take composers born before 1900 and {{contrast.comp_europe_pct|pc}} of them were born in Europe, led by Vienna ({{contrast.composers_pre1900.0.n|int}}) and Paris ({{contrast.composers_pre1900.1.n|int}}), then London, Prague, Berlin, Naples and Barcelona. Take pop, rock and metal musicians born since 1940 and Europe's share falls to {{contrast.poprock_europe_pct|pc}}: London ({{contrast.poprock_post1940.0.n|int}}) leads, then New York and Los Angeles, with Tokyo, Stockholm and Jakarta in the top ten.

    04 · Home townsMusic cities, genre by genre

    Most genres are everywhere, but some have a capital. Of the country music acts on the map, the most were born or formed in Nashville ({{signature.by.country music.n|int}}), and Nashville's share of its own acts that play country is {{genre_cities.country.lq.0.lq|f1}} times the world average. Kingston has {{signature.by.reggae.pct|pc}} of all reggae acts, Seoul {{signature.by.K-pop.pct|pc}} of K-pop, Rio de Janeiro {{signature.by.samba.pct|pc}} of samba, Buenos Aires {{signature.by.tango.pct|pc}} of tango and Lisbon {{signature.by.fado.pct|pc}} of fado. Black metal's capital is Oslo ({{signature.by.black metal.n|int}} acts), with Bergen second ({{signature.by.black metal.second_n|int}}); grunge's is Seattle; grime's is London.

    The home town of each genre

    The city area with most acts (musicians born and bands formed) tagged with each genre in Wikidata, and its share of all such acts on the map. Click a row to see the genre's family on the map.

    The location quotient tells a similar story from the other side: how much more common a family of genres is among a city's acts than among all acts. Kingston's acts are {{genre_cities.reggae.lq.0.lq|f1}} times as likely to be in reggae and its relatives as the world's, Nashville's {{genre_cities.country.lq.0.lq|f1}} times as likely to be country, New Orleans's {{genre_cities.jazz.lq.0.lq|f1}} times as likely to be jazz. Detroit stands out for soul and R&B ({{genre_cities.rnb.lq.1.lq|f1}}), Atlanta for hip hop ({{genre_cities.hiphop.lq.0.lq|f1}}).

    05 · BandsWhere bands begin

    Wikidata has {{source.g_n|int}} musical groups. It knows the country of {{source.g_iso_pct|pc}} of them, but the town where they formed for only {{source.g_loc_pct|pc}}, so the band map is thinner than the musicians' map, and it is young: {{decades.b_since1960_pct|pc}} of groups with a known start were formed in 1960 or later, with most in the {{decades.b_peak|raw}}s. The United States accounts for {{countries.us_b_pct|pc}} of bands with a country, against {{countries.us_pct|pc}} of musicians.

    Per head, the Nordic countries are the band countries. Finland has {{countries.fin.b_per_m|f1}} bands in Wikidata per million people, Norway {{countries.nor.b_per_m|f1}} and Sweden {{countries.swe.b_per_m|f1}}. For metal the gap is wider: {{countries.metal_per_m.0.metal_per_m|f1}} metal bands per million in Finland, {{countries.metal_per_m.1.metal_per_m|f1}} in Sweden and {{countries.metal_per_m.2.metal_per_m|f1}} in Norway, against {{countries.deu.metal_per_m|f1}} in Germany and {{countries.usa.metal_per_m|f1}} in the United States. Some of that is real, and some is the work of keen Nordic Wikipedia editors.

    Sort musicians by the decade they were born in and the map moves. Of musicians born before 1900, {{decades.europe_early_pct|pc}} were born in Europe and {{decades.asia_early_pct|f1}}% in Asia. Of those born since 1970, Europe's share is {{decades.europe_late_pct|pc}} and Asia's {{decades.asia_late_pct|pc}}.     07 · WomenFrom {{women.by.1700.pct|f1}}% to {{women.by.1990.pct|pc}}

    Women are {{women.pct|f1}}% of musicians whose gender Wikidata records. Of those born before 1800 they are {{women.by.1700.pct|f1}}%; through the nineteenth century the share climbs to about a fifth, and it stays there for musicians born between 1900 and the 1950s. Then it rises again: {{women.by.1970.pct|pc}} for musicians born in the 1970s, {{women.by.1980.pct|pc}} in the 1980s and {{women.by.1990.pct|pc}} in the 1990s.

    08 · CountriesThe United States, Germany and the Czech Republic

    The United States is the birth country of {{countries.top.0.n|int}} musicians, {{countries.us_pct|pc}} of everyone with a birth country, then Germany ({{countries.top.1.n|int}}), the United Kingdom ({{countries.top.2.n|int}}), Italy, France and Japan. The Czech Republic ({{countries.cze.n|int}}) is seventh, ahead of Spain and Russia. Among notable musicians the American share more than doubles, to {{countries.us_notable_pct|pc}}, and the United Kingdom is second ({{countries.top_notable.1.pct|pc}}).

    09 · BritainLiverpool, Manchester, Sheffield

    Among British cities, Liverpool comes first per head for both musicians ({{areas.liverpool.per100k|f1}} per 100,000 people living there now) and bands ({{areas.liverpool.b_per100k|f1}}), ahead of London ({{areas.london.per100k|f1}} and {{areas.london.b_per100k|f1}}).     10 · The gapsA map of who keeps records

    Notable musicians almost always have a birthplace in Wikidata ({{source.notable_bp_pct|f1}}%). For the {{source.n_sl1|int}} musicians with one sitelink or none, {{source.sl1_pct|pc}} of all, it is {{source.sl1_bp_pct|pc}}. Bands fare worse: a formation place is recorded for {{source.g_notable_pt_pct|pc}} of notable bands and {{coverage.by_sl.0.g_pt_pct|pc}} of those with one sitelink or none. Many entries came in bulk from library catalogues and music databases, which are richer for some countries (Czech, Estonian, German, Austrian) than for others, and for classical music than for popular music in much of Africa and Asia.

    11 · How I did thisMethods and caveats

    Musicians. One query through QLever returned every Wikidata human with an occupation that is musician or any of its subclasses ({{source.n_occ_values|int}} occupations appear, from composer and singer to organist, rapper, kobzar and bertsolari), with their birthplace, its coordinates and country, date of birth, gender and number of sitelinks: {{source.n_rows|int}} rows for {{source.n|int}} people, matching a separate count of the same set. I grouped the occupations into eight families and kept every family a person belongs to: {{source.n_multi_fam|int}} people are in more than one. {{source.n_nolabel|int}} have no English label in the extract and are shown by their Wikidata number.

    Bands. A second query returned every musical group, from rock bands and idol groups to choirs and orchestras ({{source.g_ens|int}} of the {{source.g_n|int}} are choirs, orchestras or ensembles, which a filter separates), with its location of formation, start and end dates, country of origin and sitelinks. A band's country is that of its formation place, else its country of origin.

    Places. A birth or formation country is the present-day country the point falls in (Natural Earth); for musicians it agrees with the place's Wikidata country {{source.agree_pct|pc}} of the time. City areas group points within 25 km of a Natural Earth city of 200,000 people or more ({{source.in_area_pct|pc}} of musicians' points), and per-head figures divide by that city's present population: a rough guide, since most of these musicians were born when cities were smaller.

    Genres. Wikidata gives a genre for {{source.genre_pct|pc}} of musicians and {{source.g_genre_pct|pc}} of bands. I grouped the {{genres.n_genre_values|int}} genres used into thirteen families by rules on their names (the full list is in the data). Genre shares are among tagged acts only.

    What this is not. It is not a census of musicians, nor a ranking of musical nations. Coverage is uneven between countries, between genres and between famous and obscure musicians. Occupations are generous: the most linked "musicians" include Leonardo da Vinci, Goethe, Martin Luther and Nietzsche, all of whom played or composed, alongside Michael Jackson, Mozart and Beethoven. Gender is as recorded, mostly as a binary ({{women.o_n|int}} people have another gender recorded), and borders are today's.
