Every musician was born somewhere, and every band started somewhere. I mapped the birthplaces of {{source.n_pt|int}} musicians and the home towns of {{source.g_pt|int}} bands in Wikidata to see which cities made them, where each genre lives, how the map moved over three centuries, and where the women are.

Wikidata, the open database behind Wikipedia, records {{source.n|int}} people whose occupation is musician or one of its many varieties: composers and singers, organists and rappers, DJs, conductors, fado singers and kobzars. It also records {{source.g_n|int}} bands, choirs, orchestras and other musical groups. For {{source.n_iso|int}} of the musicians it knows the country of birth, and for {{source.n_pt|int}} the birthplace has coordinates.

So I put them all on one map. The interactive version switches between musicians and bands, filters by kind of musician, family of genres, gender and fame, and has a decade slider you can play like a record. Click any hexagon and you get the people or bands from there, best known first.

Darker hexagons hold more musicians.}}

New York, London, Paris and Vienna

Wikidata often records a district rather than a city, Brooklyn or Hollywood or a Paris arrondissement, so I grouped every birthplace within 25 km of a city of 200,000 people or more. New York comes first with {{areas.new_york.n|int}} musicians, then London ({{areas.london.n|int}}), Paris ({{areas.paris.n|int}}) and Vienna ({{areas.vienna.n|int}}), with Tokyo the first city outside Europe and the Americas. As a single place, Vienna is the most common birthplace of all.

Bands have a different map. Los Angeles is where most bands in Wikidata were formed ({{areas.los_angeles.b|int}}), ahead of New York and London, with San Francisco and Seattle not far behind. Per head of today's population, the band towns are Ljubljana, Bergen, Malmö and Oslo, with Liverpool, the British leader on both counts, close behind.

Right, bands formed in each city: Los Angeles 1,454, New York 1,380, London 1,023, then Tokyo, San Francisco, Seoul, Jakarta and Seattle|Musicians born, and bands formed, within 25 km of each city.}}

Two maps of music

The kind of musician changes the map completely. Composers born before 1900 were {{contrast.comp_europe_pct|pc}} European: Vienna leads with {{contrast.composers_pre1900.0.n|int}}, then Paris, London, Prague, Berlin, Naples and Barcelona. Pop, rock and metal musicians born since 1940 are only {{contrast.poprock_europe_pct|pc}} European, and the map spreads to the United States, Japan, Indonesia and Brazil. Conductors are the most European kind of musician ({{contrast.fam_cont.5.Europe|pc}}); rappers are as likely to come from North America as from Europe.

Every genre has a home town

Some music is everywhere, but some has an address. Of all the K-pop acts on the map, musicians born or bands formed, {{signature.by.K-pop.pct|pc}} are from Seoul. Rio de Janeiro has {{signature.by.samba.pct|pc}} of samba, Buenos Aires {{signature.by.tango.pct|pc}} of tango, Lisbon {{signature.by.fado.pct|pc}} of fado, Hong Kong {{signature.by.cantopop.pct|pc}} of cantopop, London {{signature.by.grime.pct|pc}} of grime and Kingston {{signature.by.reggae.pct|pc}} of reggae. Black metal's capital is Oslo, with Bergen second, grunge's is Seattle and Afrobeats' is Lagos.

{{IMG|b_genres.png|Bar chart of each genre's share of acts in its top city: cantopop in Hong Kong 74 per cent, samba in Rio de Janeiro 57, grime in London 56, tango in Buenos Aires 52, K-pop in Seoul 52, fado in Lisbon 49, then sardana in Barcelona, bossa nova in Rio, trot in Seoul, chanson in Paris, J-pop in Tokyo, reggae in Kingston, flamenco in Cádiz, Carnatic music in Chennai, grunge in Seattle, Afrobeats in Lagos and dancehall in Kingston|The home town of each genre, and its share of all that genre's acts on the map.}}

Country music is the interesting exception. Country acts are spread right across the United States, so Nashville has only {{signature.by.country music.pct|pc}} of them. But turn the question round and Nashville stands out more than anywhere: its acts are {{genre_cities.country.lq.0.lq|f1}} times as likely as the world's to be country. By the same measure, New Orleans is jazz's city, Atlanta hip hop's and Detroit soul's.

Finland has {{countries.metal_per_m.0.metal_per_m|f1}} metal bands in Wikidata for every million people. The United States has {{countries.usa.metal_per_m|f1}}.

The Nordic band countries

Per head, the Nordic countries are band countries. Finland has {{countries.fin.b_per_m|f1}} bands per million people, Norway {{countries.nor.b_per_m|f1}} and Sweden {{countries.swe.b_per_m|f1}}. For metal the gap is wider still: Finland {{countries.metal_per_m.0.metal_per_m|f1}}, Sweden {{countries.metal_per_m.1.metal_per_m|f1}}, Norway {{countries.metal_per_m.2.metal_per_m|f1}}, Germany {{countries.deu.metal_per_m|f1}}. Part of that is real, a famously metal-loving region, and part is the work of keen Nordic Wikipedia editors who have written up their local scenes.

Where the women are

Women are {{women.pct|f1}}% of musicians whose gender Wikidata records. By decade of birth, the share climbs from {{women.by.1700.pct|f1}}% before 1800 to about a fifth in the nineteenth century, stays there for musicians born up to the 1950s, and then rises to {{women.by.1990.pct|pc}} for those born in the 1990s. But the kind of musician matters more than the decade: women are {{families.by.singer.w_pct|pc}} of singers and {{families.by.songwriter.w_pct|pc}} of songwriters, but {{families.by.composer.w_pct|pc}} of composers, {{families.by.rap.w_pct|pc}} of rappers, {{families.by.dj.w_pct|pc}} of DJs and producers and {{families.by.conductor.w_pct|pc}} of conductors.

Right, bars by kind of musician: singers 46 per cent, songwriters 27, other musicians 20, instrumentalists 17, composers 12, rappers 11, DJs and producers 9, conductors 7|Women's share of musicians, by decade of birth and by kind of musician.}}

A map of who keeps records

All of this rests on what Wikidata happens to know. It has a birth country for {{source.notable_bp_pct|f1}}% of famous musicians but only {{source.sl1_bp_pct|pc}} of those with one Wikipedia article or none, and a formation place for just {{source.g_loc_pct|pc}} of its bands. Whole national library catalogues have been copied in for some countries, which is why Tallinn, Prague and Brno top the per-head list of birth cities. So the map is best read as the open record of music: rich where archives and enthusiasts have filled it in, thin where they have not.

{{IMG|b_coverage.png|Bar chart by number of sitelinks: musicians with a birth country rise from 46 per cent of those with 0 or 1 sitelinks to 100 per cent of those with 50 or more; bands with a formation place on the map rise from 18 per cent to 94 per cent|How often Wikidata knows where, by how famous a musician or band is.}}

How I did this

Queries to Wikidata through the QLever service gave every person with a musical occupation, with birthplace, birth date, gender and number of sitelinks, every musical group with its place and date of formation, and the genres of both. I placed each point in its present-day country with Natural Earth outlines, grouped points within 25 km of cities of 200,000 people or more, and grouped the {{source.n_occ_values|int}} occupations into eight kinds of musician and the {{genres.n_genre_values|int}} genres into thirteen families by rules on their names. The lists are published with the data, so you can regroup them. Everything is in the paper, with the code and data on GitHub.

The usual caveats apply, and here they matter a lot. Wikidata is not a census of musicians. Its occupations are generous (the most linked "musicians" include Leonardo da Vinci, Goethe and Nietzsche, who all played or composed), it records gender mostly as a binary, genres for only {{source.genre_pct|pc}} of musicians, and its coverage depends on which catalogues have been imported. Countries use today's borders, so a composer born in Lviv in 1850 counts as Ukrainian.
